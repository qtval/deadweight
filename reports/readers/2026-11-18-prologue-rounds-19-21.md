## What the voice pass did

- **Marrow** now speaks as `story/CAST.md` says: two to seven words, no "I", the cost first ("Ten. It's me does it. And I'm sick after."). When she is frightened it all comes out in one breath. Readers now call her the best thing in the chapter, and the only distinct voice.
- **Isolde** speaks in whole sentences without contractions, and contracts only when rattled ("Don't— no."). She uses Wren's name only when taking something.
- **Fear lasts.** Wren cries after the knife, flinches when Marrow moves, takes the head of the corpse to keep it between them, drives with chin down.
- **Wren explains less.** The rope is coiled "the way he liked it coiled", with no because. Several self-diagnosing lines are gone.
- About a dozen closing one-liners were cut or flattened.
- The stakes are back on the bank in three plain sentences; at dawn Isolde says the fever plan before Marrow says "Three's a lot of us".

## Two findings that are not about sentences

**1. The First Law score follows the route.** In rounds 16 and 19 the First Law reader read the self-serving route and gave 4. On the honest and mixed routes it has been 3 every time, for six rounds. The reason given is the same: on the honest route Wren's shabbiness is "only in asides" and the player is "never made complicit". A player who chooses decently gets a less First Law chapter. That is how a choice game works, and it is not fixable by prose alone without taking the decent choices away.

**2. The phone rule and the First Law rule pull against each other.** Every First Law reader for nine rounds has asked for the same thing: room. Let the dawn scene run; let people talk past each other; let the fear go on for a page before anyone is clever. Every new player for nine rounds has said the dawn scene is where they would put the phone down, and the story test caps a stretch without a choice at 340 words. Both cannot be fully met in one scene. The dawn scene is now at the cap in every branch.
