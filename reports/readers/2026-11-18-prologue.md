## Still open (the next writing task)

1. **Temptation (g) and shabbiness on the honest route (b).** Honest choices are overruled or cost nothing visible; the selfish labels are rarely the most appealing. "It's madness" changes nothing. Make honesty cost something on the spot, and let a selfish option win something the player can see.
2. **Thought choices read as machinery.** Three readers in two rounds called "Tell yourself…/For the peace." abstract, the game asking the player to state the moral. Redesign as a concrete act that carries the excuse (for example, what Wren does with the gloves), or drop the device.
3. **One register of wit.** Every reader B and C: Aubric, Marrow, Isolde and Wren all make the same dry epigram, and most paragraphs end on a small turn. Give each a different kind of humour; leave half the paragraphs unbuttoned.
4. **Place (e).** The body is felt; the land is not. Little smell, mud or landscape beyond dark water.
5. **Isolde's plan scene** is still the place readers skim: two people argue politics while Wren watches.
6. **First Law feel stays at 3.** Reader B, each round: the head is too cool and edited, too clean (little stink, pain or swearing), nobody is sincere enough to be hurt, the stakes are told. "Closer to K. J. Parker than Abercrombie." The river scene is named each time as the one passage that has it.
7. Echoes to watch (not lifts): Aubric as a charming drunk who never pays, with a chronicler writing his legend, recalls a pairing in Red Country; a returning refrain is Abercrombie's signature device, so "Squires carry things" must stay rare; "Greywater" recalls another author's place name.
