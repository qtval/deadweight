## What round 1 taught (the plainer drafting rules overshot)

Round 1 was written strictly to the rules proposed after prologue round 9: plain paragraph endings, one wit per exchange, long uncaught excuses. The editor confirmed the neat closing turn was "largely fixed". But all three readers said the voice had gone: "Wren mostly reports", "a neutral camera", "the voice has gone polite", "reads like a careful literary historical novel". Wren's body had been reset, the narration had lost its contractions and sounded like Isolde, and the chapter's biggest moment (the lie told to two hundred people) was summarised instead of played.

So the rule is not "plainer". It is: **unbutton the paragraphs, and keep Wren's sour, bodily, self-serving thought running under every act.** Round 2 was rewritten that way and six criteria rose.

## What readers liked most (both rounds)

- "That's not him. That's a little arm."

## Still open

1. **Nobody can hurt Wren here** (h). The danger is exposure, not harm. Reader B's fix: have Dobb's kindness close on Wren inside the bridge scene, his hand going under the canvas for those feet now, in front of the crowd, so that Wren has to do something quick and ugly to stop a decent man. As written, Isolde rescues Wren and the threat is put off until night.
2. **Temptation** (g). Only the armour choice tempts. The toll choice has no selfish option and one reader could not see what not signing risked. The Marshal's riders, the prologue's closing threat, are not in Stint until scene 4.
3. **Wren still catches every shabby motive in the next sentence** ("I told myself X; it was also Y", once per scene). Reader B: "Abercrombie's people lie to themselves for longer."
4. **The wood scene has too many neat turns again** (editor); the bridge and toll are "about right".
5. **Marrow and Isolde nearly vanish after scene 1.** Their sparring was the prologue's best feature.
6. Continuity not yet carried from the prologue: the broken nose, the notched ear, where the purse went if it was left on his belt, the gloves if they were put back on his hands, the planted knot.
7. Echo to change: a veteran marked by missing fingers. Change Dobb's injury.

Fixed after round 2 without a re-read: when Wren last ate; a present-tense slip; the test harness gave the honest route a debt it had not earned.
