## What I got wrong

After round 21 I wrote that the First Law score "follows the route": 4 on the self-serving route, 3 on the others. That rested on two readings. With four more readings on that route the score is 4, 4, 4, 3, 3, 3. It is a coin toss between 3 and 4 on any route. The rule change was harmless but it was not the answer.

## What is stable across 25 rounds

- Nothing has ever been judged copied.
- Seven of the nine criteria pass every round. Temptation (g) passes about two rounds in three; it dips when one reader finds the selfish options "labelled as the bad choice".
- The First Law reader gives 3 about two times in three, and always for the same reasons: every paragraph ends on a dry line; the three living people share one clipped wit; the dawn scene (confession, plan, murder price, reprieve, consent, letter) happens in one sitting with no room; Wren sees through Wren; the war and Sayle are only names.
- **Clarity is not clean either.** Read strictly, the rule fails when two readers report the same confusion, and in every one of these rounds at least two readers could not say why the council will not sign for a dead man, why nine days, or who Sayle is. I have moved that explanation three times (the bank, the dawn dialogue, Aubric's speech). Each place draws the same complaint. Earlier reports of mine said "clarity passes"; that was too generous.

## What the readers ask for, in one line each

- First Law readers (10 of the last 12): give the dawn scene room, and break the one-sting-per-paragraph rhythm.
- New players (all): say the stakes once, plainly, early, in Wren's words; the dawn scene is where they tire.
- Editors (all): cut a third of the closing one-liners; the remember-lines read as a receipt.

## Where that leaves it (owner)

Sentence-level passes have stopped moving the scores. Three honest options:

1. **Split the dawn scene in two** (the lie and the fever plan; then, as its own scene with its own panel, the price on Wren's life and the letter), with room in each and one plain statement of the stakes at the top of the second. This is what both the First Law readers and the phone readers are asking for, and it does not need the word cap raised. It is a structural task: scene order, story spec, browser test, one more panel.
2. **Play it and pass it yourself.** The panel is a stand-in for you.
3. **Leave the prologue and write chapter 1**, where a long scene, a seen war and a present Sayle are in the beat sheet already, and come back to the prologue with what that teaches.
