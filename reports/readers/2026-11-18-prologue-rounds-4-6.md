## What changed in rounds 4–6

- **Wren's head is hotter.** "Piss on your bells", "let it rust", the thought under the crying about not rising before dawn. Readers now name these as the lines where they believed in Wren.
- **Unflagged self-deception.** Decent choices no longer confess their motive; a detail gives it away (the purse weighed "only to be sure", the buckle tried twice, the last glove slowed because Isolde is watching).
- **Aubric has room.** He rehearses his modesty and his wave, and gives Wren his gloves. The hand that comes out of the river is bare because of it. Scored 4 by every reader in rounds 5–6.
- **Lies and refusals cost on the spot.** Refuse Marrow: she takes Wren by the collar and keeps the blanket. Deny the letter: she opens Wren's shirt. Object to the plan: Wren rides under the canvas with the body. Agree: Wren is handed the signet and the blame. Bargain: a written promise. Hand over the letter: Marrow says whose neck that was.
- **The thought choice is now an act.** What Wren does with the gloves at the wrapping (put them back on him / fold his hands like the songs / keep them). No reader called it abstract after the change.
- **The plan scene is short.** The reason he must seem alive moved into Wren's own head on the bank; Isolde no longer lectures.
- **The ambush needs a wound.** Marrow makes one while Wren holds him.
- **Place and dirt.** Slop, alders, the taste of the river, sick, piss, the body leaking, gateposts robbed for arrowheads.
- Stock phrases and tics the editor listed each round were cut; several are now enforced as `voice.limits`.

After round 6 (fixed, not re-read by a panel): the third-verse plant moved into Aubric's live speech; Isolde says plainly that she knows the ambush is a story; two jokes readers did not get were cut; Marrow carries out her threat about the nose if Wren refused her and then hides the elbow; the debt-and-accounts motif was thinned; small continuity slips.

## Still open

1. **Temptation (g).** Every reader is tempted by the purse; several by the song. But keeping the letter reads as the smart move, not the selfish one, and "say nothing" at the ford feels foregone because the first sentence says he will cross. The ford choice needs a real stake, or should stop pretending to be one.
2. **First Law feel is stuck at 3.** The reader's list in round 6, in order:
   - Wren's private commentary runs hot on the first page and thins in the second half; in the Marrow and Isolde scenes Wren watches two wittier people.
   - Fear and pain do not linger. Marrow's grip is over in six lines; the elbow swells once.
   - Marrow, Isolde and Wren still share one dry register. Only Aubric has his own sound.
   - No bastard with a face. The threat is "men at court"; a First Law book would have named one petty, frightening person by now.
   - Trailer density: nearly every line is a zinger. Let scenes breathe and repeat before the knife.
   This is probably not fixable by polishing the prologue further. It needs the cast sheet (a distinct voice and verbal habit per character, a named antagonist) and longer scenes, which is M1 and chapter 1.
3. **The editor's standing note, all six rounds:** too many paragraphs end on a turn. Better than it was; still audible.
4. **The dawn scene** is still where the new player's attention dips.
5. Echoes to watch (unchanged): a charming drunk hero with a writer making his legend; a returning refrain. "Squires carry things" stays at two uses.
