## What changed in rounds 10–12

- **Decent choices cost on the page.** Leave the purse and Marrow cuts it off his belt herself ("You didn't want it. Six years, was it? I've carried him one night."). Tell Isolde the truth and she makes Wren say the lie five times, the last with a hand on him.
- **The rope.** On the bank Wren throws it short, throws it again, and then coils it a third time, neatly, the way he liked it coiled, so as not to go nearer the noise. Two readers called this the best thing in the chapter.
- **People are frightened and wrong for a moment.** Marrow says he is breathing. Isolde says "Wake him."
- **Marrow ties Wren's wrist to hers** for the night if Wren will not decide.
- **The letter's stakes in two plain sentences**, and a shorter plan scene. The Sayle flashback is one line.
- Explanatory glosses the editor listed were cut.

## Why the First Law score does not move

Twelve rounds, three different drafting approaches, the same reader verdict. In round 12's words: the narrator is "dry, observant and mostly correct", there is "no self-deception I can see past", and Wren is "a decent witness steered by two sharper women". The ask is the same each time and it is structural: **give Wren a hot, grasping want of their own that survives Aubric's death and drives the second half, something Wren schemes for, badly.** The prologue as designed has Wren reacting from the bank onward.

That is a change to the story, not to the sentences. It belongs with the owner: what does Wren want, for themself, from the corpse?

Also noted by more than one reader this round, and not fixed: the dawn scene is still where the new player tires; "He's breathing" still trips readers; the stacked remember-lines in the wrapping scene read as the game's bookkeeping; Marrow's closed hand behind Isolde is unclear; the last line was called sentimental by the editor and loved by three others.
