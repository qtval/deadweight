# Promo viewer panel

The promo (`tools/promo/`, `npm run promo`) is checked like the prose: by fresh viewers who know nothing about the project, after the automatic checks (`python3 tools/promo/review.py`) pass. At most three rounds per task. Results go in `reports/promo/panel-*.md`.

Viewers cannot watch the film, so each gets the review packet: `reports/promo/review/packet.md` (the frame list with times and the caption on screen, plus the designed sound) and the frames themselves (`reports/promo/review/f*.jpg`), which they open and look at in order.

## Viewers

- **A — New player.** Plays story games on a phone; has not read much fantasy. Sees only the packet.
- **B — First Law reader.** Has read Abercrombie's books several times; hard to impress. Sees only the packet.
- **C — The game's art director.** Sees the packet plus the game's own opening (`reports/transcripts/begin-reader-b-selfserving.md`) and the art rules below; judges whether the promo is the same game.

Art rules C checks: black-and-white ink and charcoal; amber only for real light (lanterns, flames, lit windows); no faces; no text inside the pictures (captions laid over them are allowed, like the game's text box); figures small, from behind or in silhouette; nothing that contradicts the game.

## Questions

Part 1, from memory, one or two sentences each: (1) What is the game about: who is the narrator, what happened, what is at stake? (2) Who are the other people shown and what do they want? (3) What would you expect to do as the player? (4) Anything confusing (a picture you could not read, a line, a name)? List each or say "nothing".

Part 2, scores 1–5 with one line of reason each:

- p. I understand the premise: who, what happened, and what is at stake.
- q. I want to play this.
- r. It feels grim and darkly funny, not generic fantasy.
- s. The pictures look like one deliberate style, and I can read what each shows.
- t. The words fit the pictures and the pace.
- u. (C only) It matches the game: tone, facts, voice and art rules.

Part 3, per viewer: A — where would you have stopped watching? B — how close is the feel to a First Law book (1–5), and does anything read as lifted (character, name, phrase, mantra, plot beat)? C — list every rule break or contradiction with the game, or "none".

Part 4: the three weakest moments and the three strongest. Part 5: the single change that would improve it most.

## Pass rule

A round passes when p–t each average 4 or more with no score below 3, B's First Law feel is 4 or more, nothing is lifted, C gives u 4 or more and lists no rule break or contradiction, and no confusion in Part 1 item 4 is reported by two viewers. The promo passes on two consecutive passing rounds of the same film.
